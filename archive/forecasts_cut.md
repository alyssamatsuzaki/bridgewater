## Original Forecast 3 — cut

**Reason:** a domestic transit program that touches neither mercantilism nor AI except by analogy to "physical delivery," which forecasts on datacenter cancellations, capex, and depreciation (originals 8, 9, 12) already test inside the AI buildout itself. Its figure (`figures_cut/fig06_lametro.png`) went with it.

**Original text:**

> **3. Fewer than 20 of the 28 projects in LA Metro's Twenty-eight by '28 program will be open for passenger revenue service on July 14, 2028. Probability 70% (leaning).**
> Resolution: point in time on the Games' opening date. The project list is the 28 as revised by Metro's March 2024 board action, which replaced eleven original projects with achievable substitutes; that revised list governs. "Open" means carrying fare-paying passengers on the segment named in the program list; groundbreakings and substantial-completion milestones do not count, and a partial segment counts only if revenue service runs on it. Strictly fewer than 20; exactly 20 resolves NO. Metro's own press releases and board reports govern, first print; fallback is the FTA's quarterly project status reporting. Openings are announced in real time, so the data exists by the date.
> The linkage: announced capacity and delivered capacity decouple wherever the binding constraint is physical delivery rather than money.

The Part 3 derivation ("Forecast 3 (LA28)") is preserved in `original/submission.md`.

## Original Forecast 14 — cut

**Reason:** unanimous reviewer flag; its own resolution language concedes the trigger is "mechanism-blind" — a generic carry unwind resolves YES without mercantilism or AI appearing anywhere in the causal chain.

**Original text:**

> **14. Between August 3, 2026 and December 31, 2027, USD/JPY will move at least 10% peak-to-trough, in either direction, within a single window of 20 consecutive trading days. Probability 30% (contest).**
> Resolution: daily USD/JPY from the Federal Reserve's H.10 release (FRED series DEXJPUS), first print; for every window of 20 consecutive H.10 trading days beginning on or after August 3, 2026, compute (window maximum minus window minimum) divided by window minimum; any window at or above 10.0% resolves YES, and exactly 10.0% resolves YES. Fallback if the H.10 series is discontinued: Bank of Japan 17:00 JST spot rates, same rule.
> The linkage: when trade flows are set administratively, the exchange rate becomes the residual adjustment for whatever policy does not control, and yen funding sits under a meaningful share of the leverage financing the AI buildout, so a disorderly yen move is one of the ways the delivery constraint gets marked to market early. The reference-class event is the August 2024 carry unwind, roughly 12% in five weeks; with USD/JPY at multi-decade highs and the BoJ tightening from 1% while intervention risk stays live, we price a repeat within seventeen months at three in ten, roughly double the unconditional base rate. The resolution is deliberately mechanism-blind: a generic carry unwind also resolves YES, so the framework reading claims only co-movement with forecasts 8, 9, and 12.

## Original Forecast 16 — cut

**Reason:** sits one causal link past the core-PCE forecast (mercantilist inflation → FOMC reaction function → point-in-time rate level), and its entire NO case is a recession unrelated to the framework; original 15 prices the same claim one step closer to the mechanism.

**Original text:**

> **16. On December 31, 2027, the upper bound of the FOMC's federal funds target range will be at or above 3.75%, its level as of July 30, 2026. Probability 55% (contest).**
> Resolution: the target range set by the FOMC's most recent policy implementation note in effect on that date; FRED series DFEDTARU (target range upper limit) is the reference record, first print; point in time. An upper bound of exactly 3.75% resolves YES. If the FOMC replaces the target range with a single target rate, that rate is compared to 3.75% under the same rule.
> The linkage: the reaction function is where the framework's two forces collide. Mercantilist inflation (forecast 15) argues for holding or hiking; the delivery constraint's growth drag, with Q2 2026 real GDP at a 1.5% annualized rate, argues for cutting. The July 2026 FOMC held at 3.50-3.75% for a fifth consecutive meeting on a 9-3 vote with the dissents favoring a hike, dropped the cut its projections had carried, and left markets pricing roughly even odds of a hike by year-end, with the 30-year Treasury already through 5.2%, its highest since 2007. 55% says inflation wins the argument through 2027; the other 45% is the recession that forces fast cuts, which is exactly the scenario in which forecasts 8 and 9 also fire.

## Original Forecast 18 — cut

**Reason:** the longest market chain in the set (AI → value migration → sector rotation → index returns net of concentration effects), and the draft itself conceded index concentration can swamp the thesis in either direction — a "most direct market test" that cannot attribute its own outcome.

**Original text:**

> **18. The calendar-2027 total return of the S&P 500 Information Technology sector will be below the arithmetic average of the calendar-2027 total returns of the S&P 500 Energy, Industrials, and Utilities sectors. Probability 45% (contest).**
> Resolution: S&P Dow Jones Indices official sector total return index levels, December 31, 2026 close to December 31, 2027 close, first published values; the Information Technology return is compared to the simple average of the other three. Fallback if S&P discontinues the sector total return indices: NAV total returns of XLK against the average of XLE, XLI, and XLU over the same dates; if both are unavailable, the forecast is unresolved.
> The linkage: the value-migration claim of Part 2 section 4, stated as a market price. If AI compresses the value of codified output while watts, trades, and grid position bind, equity returns should rotate from the sector selling the codified good toward the sectors that own the physical constraint. Consensus momentum runs the other way, and index concentration lets a handful of names swamp the thesis in either direction, which is why the framework's most direct market test gets 45% rather than a conviction number.
