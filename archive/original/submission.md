# Forecasting the Future 2026: Submission

*Modern mercantilism suppresses price signals in exchange for control of strategic inputs; AI reprices sensing and codified output. The eighteen forecasts below, the framework in Part 2, and the derivations in Part 3 are one claim priced eighteen ways: the decade belongs to systems that take control without losing error correction.*

# Part 1: Forecasts

Eighteen forecasts follow: two near-certain structural claims, seven leaning calls, and nine genuine contests, three of which sit between 40% and 60% because that is where the evidence puts them. Every probability is constructed the same way. We start from a reference-class or market-implied prior, measure the distance between the current state and the threshold, and adjust for the named drivers and countervailing pressures; Part 3 states the prior and the adjustment wherever one exists. Forecasts 15 through 18 translate the framework into the macro-financial variables it implies: inflation, the policy rate, tariff revenue, and sector returns. Ten figures carry the underlying series; Figure 1 maps the full set.

**1. More than 60% of new passenger vehicles sold at retail in China in calendar 2027 will be new energy vehicles (battery-electric plus plug-in hybrid). Probability 85% (structural).**
Resolution: China Passenger Car Association full-year 2027 retail data, published each January without interruption; share equals NEV retail units divided by total passenger-vehicle retail units, aggregated over the calendar year (no single month resolves it). First published print governs; later revisions are ignored. Strictly greater than 60.0%; exactly 60.0% resolves NO. Fallback if CPCA discontinues the series: CAAM wholesale data, same threshold and rules.
The linkage: policy has pushed the EV transition past the point where the suppressed price signal (unsubsidized relative cost) still binds. The forecast tests whether the transition now runs on its own.

**2. On December 31, 2027, the published on-demand output-token price of the most capable generally available model from at least one of OpenAI, Anthropic, or Google will be at least 50% below that same provider's most-capable-model output price on July 31, 2026. Probability 90% (structural).**
Resolution: two point-in-time snapshots of each provider's official API pricing page (Wayback Machine copies dated nearest each date). "Most capable" means the model the provider's own pricing page designates as its top general-purpose tier; successors and renamed models count. Standard on-demand pricing only; batch, cached, and negotiated rates are excluded. A drop of exactly 50.0% resolves YES. The archived pages are the source of truth, so resolution by the date is certain; fallback is official pricing announcements on provider blogs.
The linkage: AI collapses the price of codified output first, and the models' own product is the most codified good in the economy.

**3. Fewer than 20 of the 28 projects in LA Metro's Twenty-eight by '28 program will be open for passenger revenue service on July 14, 2028. Probability 70% (leaning).**

The project list is the 28 as revised by Metro's March 2024 board action, which replaced eleven original projects with achievable substitutes; that revised list governs. "Open" means carrying fare-paying passengers on the segment named in the program list; groundbreakings and substantial-completion milestones do not count, and a partial segment counts only if revenue service runs on it. Strictly fewer than 20; exactly 20 resolves NO. Metro's own press releases and board reports govern, first print; fallback is the FTA's quarterly project status reporting. Openings are announced in real time, so the data exists by the date.
The linkage: announced capacity and delivered capacity decouple wherever the binding constraint is physical delivery rather than money.

The July 2026 FOMC held at 3.50-3.75% for a fifth consecutive meeting on a 9-3 vote with the dissents favoring a hike, dropped the cut its projections had carried, and left markets pricing roughly even odds of a hike by year-end, with the 30-year Treasury already through 5.2%, its highest since 2007. 55% says inflation wins the argument through 2027; the other 45% is the recession that forces fast cuts, which is exactly the scenario in which forecasts 8 and 9 also fire.

Consensus momentum runs the other way, and index concentration lets a handful of names swamp the thesis in either direction, which is why the framework's most direct market test gets 45% rather than a conviction number.

# Part 2: Framework

*Modern mercantilism trades the price mechanism for control of strategic inputs. AI collapses the cost of sensing and the price of codified output. What decides the decade is which systems take control while keeping their error correction intact.*

Institutions run a feedback loop. They sense conditions, store what happened, predict, act, and then sense the results, either updating or accumulating error. Modern Mercantilism is a deliberate rewiring of that loop: a government dulls certain feedback signals, relative prices above all, in exchange for direct control over strategic inputs. What separates the outcomes is whether error correction survives somewhere else in the loop once it has been suppressed in the price mechanism. AI enters because it changes the cost of sensing. Telemetry and real-time measurement become cheap; some older signals go obsolete. So the deciding question of the next decade is which systems acquire strategic control while keeping their error correction intact, and which mistake control for competence. Winners direct capital while preserving internal contestation. Losers achieve command and lose the ability to detect that they are wrong.

**One epistemological commitment sits under everything that follows: markets misprice regime change systematically**, because consensus is anchored to the prior regime's cause-and-effect relationships and updates only after the new relationships have produced visible outcomes. That is why half of our forecasts take positions against consensus rather than shading toward the base rate. If the regime is changing, a base rate constructed from the prior regime is the wrong prior. The table below maps the framework to the forecast set; the derivations sit in Part 3.

| Framework claim | Forecasts that price it | Where we differ most from what is priced in |
|---|---|---|
| The return on strategic control arrives in crises, as optionality | 1, 4, 5, 13, 17 | The IEA has global demand rebounding 2 mb/d in 2027; we bet China's gasoline line does not participate (4) |
| The binding constraint has moved from capital to physical delivery | 3, 8, 9, 12, 14 | No published estimate models a 2028 hyperscaler capex contraction; we hold 30% (9) |
| Workflow data is the new chokepoint, and employment law sets its accumulation rate | 7, 10 | Data marketplaces get predicted constantly; we put 70% on the moat outcome (10) |
| When codified knowledge gets cheap, value migrates to what stays scarce | 2, 6, 11, 18 | China loses the frontier scoreboard while winning the diffusion scoreboard (11 and 6 as a pair) |
| The chain ends in an inflation floor and a squeezed reaction function | 15, 16, 17 | Consensus has core returning toward 2% with cuts resuming; we price a hold-or-hike regime at 55% (15, 16) |

**1. The return on mercantilism arrives during crises, as optionality.** The standard scoreboard for industrial policy, market share and trade balances, measures the wrong output. The return on strategic control is invisible most of the time and pays during crises. China's EV program is the worked example. A policy sold as auto-sector competitiveness turned out to be energy security: by the time the Hormuz disruption hit this spring, the electric fleet had already displaced roughly a million barrels a day of oil demand, and China could pull roughly four million barrels a day out of normal purchasing (crude imports fell about 40%) while cutting refinery runs and curtailing product exports, as Brent round-tripped from above $113 in late March to under $70 by mid-June. Demand elasticity was the shock absorber, and it had been built years earlier at subsidized cost. Forecasts 1 and 4 are the continuation bets: the transition no longer needs the subsidy, and the oil-demand break holds through the post-shock rebound.

![Figure 2. CPCA retail data. The unsubsidized share crossed 54% for full-year 2025 and touched 60% in a single month for the first time in December. Forecast 1 asks the full year to get there by 2027.](figs/fig02_nev_share.png)

The comparison cases run the same loop with worse error correction. ASEAN's China+1 buildout was funded by a tariff arbitrage the July Section 301 action just compressed (forecast 13), while Korea and Japan hold capital and process knowledge without the demographics. The discriminating observation is on the record: Thailand, at one-fifth of Japan's income per head, put battery-electrics at 19.4% of new-car sales in 2025, with Chinese brands holding three-quarters of that market; Japan, five times richer, sat near 2%. Income predicts the opposite of what happened. Policy-priced access predicts it exactly, and when Japanese adoption finally jumped in early 2026, a subsidy program moved it.

![Figure 3. Plug-in share of new-car sales against income, 2025. If adoption followed wealth, the points would slope upward. They sort instead on policy-priced access to Chinese models, which is the claim.](figs/fig03_income_scatter.png)

**2. The binding constraint has moved from capital to physical delivery.** Money is not scarce; transformers, interconnection queues, skilled trades, and permitting throughput are. LA is the cleanest natural experiment, because Olympic deadlines cannot slip: of twenty-eight transit projects on an eleven-year runway with dedicated tax funding, eleven were complete by May 2026 on Metro's own tracker, roughly a quarter of the list was still in planning in late 2025, and the board had already swapped out eleven of the original projects in 2024 as undeliverable, which is announced capacity redefined rather than delivered (forecast 3).

![Figure 4. The burn-up the deadline forces. Resolution NO needs nine more openings in twenty-six months, almost exactly the recent run rate, on a list already revised once for deliverability.](figs/fig06_lametro.png)

Now hold that delivery record against roughly $700 billion of calendar-2026 capex guided by Microsoft, Alphabet, Amazon, and Meta alone, drawing on the same national pool of transformers and electricians, and in Southern California on the same regional grid and trades. Announced and energized capacity have decoupled, and the recognition of that gap is itself forecastable. Forecast 8 prices a disclosed gigawatt-scale cancellation, forecast 9 a falling capex print, forecast 12 a shortened server-depreciation schedule, and forecast 14 the funding-side tremor: yen borrowing carries a meaningful share of the buildout's leverage, so a disorderly move in the currency is one of the ways the constraint gets marked to market early. Rare earths are the same constraint one level down. Beijing's export-control listings plus a formalized enforcement bounty currently decide who manufactures the permanent magnets in every humanoid actuator (forecast 5).

![Figure 5. Four companies' purchases of property and equipment, calendar years. Guidance for 2026 is roughly triple the 2024 outturn. Forecast 9 prices the first down year against a consensus that has none.](figs/fig05_capex.png)

**3. Workflow data is the new chokepoint, and employment law now sets its accumulation rate.** The scarce input for frontier AI has moved from public text to proprietary workflow data: recordings of how humans actually operate software. That data does not exist on the open internet. It exists inside firms, generated by employees, which makes its accumulation a question of labor and privacy law, and therefore of trade. Nobody is calling it trade policy yet. Meta's Model Capability Initiative is the test case: screen content and keystroke-level activity captured from US employees with no broad opt-out, halted after a security review exposed the captured data internally. Meta paused the program; it did not cancel it, so the infrastructure and the corpus both survive. The US permits this accumulation. The EU largely does not, through GDPR's minimization rules, national workplace-monitoring statutes, and the case law that Thread 2 details. So the accumulation rate of the decade's scarce input is set by employment regulation, and Europe may be regulating itself out of the input market while believing it is protecting citizens. That tradeoff is real, and it is why we expect enforcement to surface (forecast 7) while the input itself stays locked inside firms as a moat rather than becoming a traded commodity (forecast 10).

**4. When codified knowledge gets cheap, value migrates to what stays scarce.** AI compresses the value of codified knowledge, and the repricing flows toward tacit skill and physical position. The compression is visible first in the price of the models' own output (forecast 2), and it makes inference demand less sticky than the capex assumes, because routing work across several interchangeable models is exactly what you do with a codified good. The unit economic of the whole buildout is token consumption per completed task, and almost nobody writes about it.

![Figure 6. OpenAI's flagship output price as the cleanest public series: an 83% decline in twenty-nine months. Forecast 2 needs any one of three providers to repeat half of that in seventeen.](figs/fig09_tokens.png)

The same repricing explains where automation actually lands. Consensus says automation follows labor cost. We say it follows labor scarcity, which is why checkout-free retail appears in aging, tight-labor-market societies before it appears where labor is cheap. The discriminating record: Japan, where the working-age population has shrunk for twelve straight years, had self-checkout in 77.1% of supermarkets by 2025 on the Japan Supermarket Association's annual survey, with convenience majors running unmanned formats they themselves attribute to the inability to hire; Korea's unmanned-shop count has grown into the tens of thousands. The control case ran the other way. Amazon's checkout-free grocery format in the high-wage US market leaned, by press accounts Amazon disputed in degree rather than kind, on more than a thousand India-based reviewers labeling video, and was pulled from full-size US groceries in 2024. Where labor is abundant, the rational move was to use the humans; where it is disappearing, staff-less formats scale. A second, underweighted mechanism follows: removing client-facing staff deletes an error-correction layer, and the cost lands on customers as unpriced labor, which predicts partial reversals in specific sectors rather than a monotonic march. China's position is a split verdict by design: compute controls hold it off the frontier leaderboard (forecast 11) while low deployment friction wins it diffusion into physical industry (forecast 6).

![Figure 7. IFR installation shares. China has installed a majority of the world's industrial robots since 2021 and cleared the 54% threshold in 2024. Forecast 6 asks the trend to hold roughly flat while the frontier scoreboard (forecast 11) stays out of reach.](figs/fig08_robots.png)

**5. The macro block follows from the chain.** The chain the forecasts trace runs in one direction: tariffs, subsidies, export controls, and enforcement bounties move relative prices; repriced inputs redirect investment toward strategic capacity and away from measured efficiency; the investment mix sets the growth and inflation blend; the blend forces the fiscal and monetary response; and that response is what markets finally price. Forecasts 15 through 18 read the gauges along that chain: the inflation floor (15), the reaction function it squeezes (16), the wall's own revenue line (17), and the equity rotation that value migration implies (18). The term premium shows up inside 16's derivation, and the yen forecast (14) is the same chain read from the funding side.

**How we would know we are wrong.** A single miss would not do it; the tests are patterns. If capex compounds through 2028 with energized capacity keeping pace (forecasts 8, 9, and 12 all NO), the delivery constraint is imaginary. If Chinese gasoline demand rebounds above its shock-year level (4 NO) while the EV share stalls (1 NO), the invulnerability account of mercantilism fails its best case. If a Chinese lab takes and holds the frontier crown while diffusion stalls (11 YES and 6 NO), control and competence travel together and our central distinction is wrong. If a liquid workflow-data market emerges quickly and broadly (10 YES by 2028), the chokepoint framing dies. The macro block carries its own kill switch: if core spends 2027 below 3% while the Fed cuts (15 and 16 both NO), the mercantilist inflation floor is imaginary and the transmission from policy wedge to prices fails at the first link. Any three of those together would be fatal. The forecast set is deliberately built so the framework can lose.

# Part 3: Analytical Appendix

*Derivations for the positions where arithmetic carries the argument, and a self-audit of where we are most likely to be wrong.*

## Thread 1: China's Oil Position Is Arithmetic, and the Crisis Tested It

The claim in Part 2 section 1 rests on arithmetic that should be shown. China's NEV fleet stood at 31.4 million vehicles at the end of 2024 (Ministry of Public Security registration data, January 2025). Assume the MPS split of 70.3% battery-electric, a blended 13,000 km per vehicle-year (private cars run lower; taxi and ride-hail fleets, which electrified first, run far higher), and counterfactual ICE economy of 7.5 L/100km. Battery-electrics then displace about 21 to 22 billion litres of gasoline a year; plug-in hybrids at half electric mileage add roughly 4 billion more. Call it 26 billion litres, about 160 million barrels a year, or roughly 0.45 million barrels a day of gasoline from passenger cars alone. Electric buses, two- and three-wheelers, and LNG trucking displace middle distillates on top of that, which is how the widely cited figure of about one million barrels a day of total displacement (consistent with Kpler's estimate of roughly 540 kb/d of gasoline and 500 kb/d of diesel displaced in 2026) becomes credible rather than promotional.

The crisis mechanics matter more than the level. With world supply 10 to 13.6 mb/d below pre-conflict levels from March into May on the IEA's monthly accounting, China pulled roughly four million barrels a day out of normal purchasing (crude imports down about 40% on IEA logs) while cutting refinery runs and curtailing product exports; the price path is in Part 2 section 1. The mechanism is that demand elasticity is a form of storage. Decomposed honestly, the fleet's structural displacement is the smaller share of that swing; refinery-run cuts and halted product exports did the volume, with inventory draws behind both. But each of those levers can be pulled without domestic shortage only because baseline gasoline demand is structurally lower and falling. The fleet did not supply the swing volume itself. It was the option that made the swing survivable, bought years earlier at subsidized prices while the trade-balance scoreboard recorded only the cost.

![Figure 8. The left panel is the displacement arithmetic from this thread; the right panel is what it bought during the Hormuz disruption. The fleet supplied the smaller share of the swing and made the rest of it survivable.](figs/fig04_oil.png)

Gasoline demand is tracking a 5.5% decline this year (GL Consulting via Bloomberg; the same consultancy expected 5.2% before the war), but the IEA's vintages show 2025 flat against 2024, so the shock year has not yet been tested for durability. Forecast 4 therefore asks the sharpest version of the question, as a ratchet rather than a threshold: does the decline hold once the price pressure that helped produce it is gone? Any year-over-year rebound at all resolves it NO, which is why it sits at 65% rather than higher. Displacement from a fleet compounding at double-digit rates runs against induced driving across an ICE fleet only now reaching its peak.

The same loop with weaker error correction produces the ASEAN fork. The China+1 relocation was funded by a tariff differential, and the July 23 Section 301 action, hitting sixty economies at 10% or 12.5% (Vietnam and Thailand at the 12.5% tier, with product exemptions for goods the US cannot source elsewhere; USTR, July 23, 2026), compresses it. The race in forecast 13 is between two rates: how fast the wall closes and how fast relocated capacity becomes genuinely productive rather than a transshipment veneer. We give the wall only 38% because relocation has out-run every previous tariff round, and because the automation alternative shows up in forecast 6's robot numbers either way. The two forecasts triangulate: if 13 resolves NO while 6 resolves YES, capacity automated in place, and the demographic-dividend story of ASEAN needs rewriting.

## Thread 2: Workflow Data Is a Chokepoint, and Employment Law Is the Trade Policy

The legal asymmetry is checkable today. In the US, employer monitoring of company equipment is broadly lawful under the business-purpose exception to federal wiretap law, with notice statutes in a handful of states and no federal ban. In the EU, four instruments bite before any AI-specific rule: GDPR's minimization and purpose-limitation principles, Italy's Workers' Statute Article 4 (monitoring only for narrow authorized purposes with union or inspectorate approval), German case law confining keystroke logging to credible suspicion of serious crime, and works-council codetermination vetoes over monitoring systems. The AI Act's general application from 2 August 2026 puts workplace AI systems in the high-risk tier from that date. None of this was written as industrial policy. All of it now functions as industrial policy, because it sets the accumulation rate of the input that agentic models need most.

Why is workflow data the scarce input at all? Because agentic performance depends on demonstrations of how humans actually operate software, and those demonstrations do not exist publicly at scale. The progression has run from scraped public text to licensed corpora to recorded human workflow, each step advantaging whoever combines regulatory permission with a captive population generating the data. Meta's MCI is what that looks like in practice: capture at the operating-system level with no broad opt-out, and a June 23 pause, publicly confirmed by Meta, after the captured corpus (by press accounts including private conversations, performance records, tax filings, and medical data) was found exposed across roughly 45,000 internal tables, with more than 1,600 employees petitioning cancellation. Paused, and the distinction matters: the corpus exists, the incentive survives the scandal, and where law permits collection a pause is an interruption. Forecast 7 watches the enforcement side of the same collision.

The genuine contest is forecast 10, the moat-versus-market question, and our 30% deserves defense since data marketplaces get predicted constantly. What holds the market back: Arrow's information problem first, since a buyer cannot value workflow data without inspecting it, and inspection transfers the value. Adverse selection second: a firm willing to sell its workflow exhaust signals that nothing operationally distinctive is in it, so offered supply is systematically the least valuable supply. Liability third, because the seller cannot cleanly separate employee personal data from process data, so every sale imports the buyer's regulatory exposure. And strategic retention closes the loop: a firm that understands what its workflow data teaches will rent the fruits (fine-tuned internal models) and keep the corpus. If we are wrong, the failure mode is visible early. A broker layer emerges that certifies and anonymizes workflow corpora, the way clinical-claims aggregators eventually standardized healthcare data, and the first Fortune 500 seller outside tech gets a price that makes the adverse-selection argument look precious. Forecast 10 watches for exactly that, on a deliberately long window.

Europe closes the thread. GDPR and the AI Act set European firms' accumulation rate at a fraction of their US and Chinese rivals'. The comfortable reading is that Europe rents US-trained models. The uncomfortable reading, which we hold at moderate confidence, is that agentic competence is process-specific, so Europe imports operational practice along with software. Forecast 7 is the near-term observable that the collision is real.

## Shorter Derivations

**Forecast 3 (LA28).** Metro's tracker showed nine of the twenty-eight complete in December 2025 and eleven by May 2026, on the list as revised in March 2024, a revision that itself replaced eleven original projects that could not make the deadline. Resolving NO requires at least nine more openings in roughly twenty-four months. The recent run rate, two openings in five months, extrapolates to almost exactly that; against it, the completions so far were the achievable substitutes, roughly a quarter of the list was still in planning in late 2025, and Metro's rail openings have historically slipped two to three years. The prior draft priced this at 80%; the tracker's movement since December is the kind of evidence that should move a probability, so it now sits at 70%. Deadline-driven scope cuts (opening truncated segments and counting them) remain the known gaming route, which the resolution language closes.

**Forecast 6 (robots).** IFR World Robotics 2024 puts China at 276,288 of 541,302 global installations in 2023, or 51.0%; the 2025 edition puts 2024 at 295,000 of 542,076, or 54.4%. The threshold of 54% therefore asks the trend to hold roughly flat, and 68% prices a real chance of a Chinese capex air-pocket rather than doubt about direction.

**Forecast 12 (depreciation).** The recent history runs one way: Microsoft extended server lives to six years (announced 2022), Alphabet to six years (January 2023), Amazon to six years (January 2024), Meta to five and a half (January 2025); Amazon then reduced lives for a subset of servers and network equipment effective January 2025, citing AI hardware turnover, with a $920 million accelerated-depreciation charge (all disclosed in SEC filings and earnings communications, 2022-2025). The income sensitivity is mechanical: on $100 billion of gross server assets, moving straight-line depreciation from six years back to four adds roughly $8 billion of annual expense. That is why we exclude Amazon (already resolved), require two companies, and still price only 30%: the earnings incentive to delay recognition is enormous, and the accounting rules allow delay far longer than the economics deserve.

![Figure 10. Four years of extensions, then the first reversal. Amazon's January 2025 reduction is what recognition looks like; forecast 12 asks whether two more of the four follow by March 2029.](figs/fig07_depreciation.png)

**Forecast 9 (capex).** The contest is timing. The physical constraint is real now; the accounting recognition (forecast 12) and the disclosure of cancellations (forecast 8) are the leading indicators; an actual year-over-year contraction of the four-company sum is the trailing confirmation, and 2028 may be a year early. We hold 30% because a framework built on suppressed feedback signals should put real weight on the signal reconnecting, and because if we truly believe forecasts 8 and 12 at 65% and 30%, conditional coherence forces this number well above the effectively-zero weight published estimates assign. The reference class for late-then-sudden recognition is the 2000-02 telecom buildout, where US carrier capex fell by roughly half from its peak within three years of the same announced-versus-delivered gap closing.

## Self-Audit: Stress-Testing Our Own Positions

**Falsifiability of the major Part 2 claims, within three years.** The Part 2 falsification block states the pattern-level tests. Two additions at the data level: workflow-data-as-chokepoint is falsified if frontier agentic gains by 2028 are demonstrably driven by synthetic data and RL environments with no observed bidding for human workflow corpora; value migrating to tacit and physical assets is falsified if BLS occupational wage data through 2028 shows codified-knowledge occupations holding relative wages while skilled-trade premia flatten.

**The three claims most likely to be wrong.**
(1) Forecast 9, the capex contraction: the constraint can be real and the contraction still land in 2029 or 2030, making us wrong on timing while right on mechanism, and the forecast scores the timing. (2) Forecast 4's ratchet: if post-shock price normalization induces enough extra driving across the remaining ICE fleet to outpace the growing fleet's displacement in a single year, 2027 prints above 2026 and the forecast resolves NO while the decade-scale thesis stays intact. That would be a timing loss on the mechanism we most believe in, which is why it is a leaning call and the series we would watch monthly. (3) Forecast 16, the Fed hold: a 2027 recession produces fast cuts and a NO even if the inflation mechanism is right, making it, like forecast 9, a timing bet on when the squeeze resolves.

**The three hardest to defend against a hostile expert.**
(1) The moat call in forecast 10, because the adverse-selection argument is theory with thin direct evidence, and a data-market specialist can cite functioning precedents (ad data, credit data, healthcare claims) where exactly such markets formed once brokers standardized the asset. (2) Automation-follows-scarcity, because a development economist can point to Vietnamese and Thai factories automating rapidly for quality and FDI reasons that have nothing to do with labor scarcity. Our counter is the largest automation event on record: China installs 54% of the world's industrial robots at manufacturing wages a fraction of US or Korean levels, in the same years its working-age population has been shrinking, and the provincial machine-substitution subsidy programs that seeded the boom were framed, in their own documents and coverage, around recruitment shortfalls in the Pearl River Delta rather than wage levels. If cost were the driver, the robots would go where labor is expensive; they are going where labor is disappearing. (3) The claim that Europe is regulating itself out of the input market, because the expert reply, that Europe rents frontier capability and GDPR-compliant telemetry still accumulates at scale, is partly right, and our rebuttal depends on process-specificity of agentic competence, which is an empirical question nobody has settled.

**Conviction lines.** The position we most want to defend live: the invulnerability reading of China's EV-oil position, because the derivation is arithmetic and the crisis behavior is on the record. The one we least want to defend: forecast 9, because it is a timing bet against the deepest-pocketed capex programs in history.
