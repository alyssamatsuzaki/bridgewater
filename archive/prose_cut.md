# Prose removed in the July 2026 revision

Passages cut from surviving sections, with reasons. The complete original text is in `original/submission.md`; this file records the deliberate removals that were not simple compression.

## 1. Conviction-label taxonomy (Part 1 intro and every forecast header)

**Removed:** "Eighteen forecasts follow: two near-certain structural claims, seven leaning calls, and nine genuine contests, three of which sit between 40% and 60% because that is where the evidence puts them." — plus the "(structural)", "(leaning)", "(contest)" tags on all eighteen forecasts, and the taxonomy-keyed colors in the figures.

**Reason:** ruling — the probabilities already carry conviction; a second, undefined confidence vocabulary reads as rhetoric.

## 2. Epistemological-commitment paragraph (Part 2)

**Removed:**

> **One epistemological commitment sits under everything that follows: markets misprice regime change systematically**, because consensus is anchored to the prior regime's cause-and-effect relationships and updates only after the new relationships have produced visible outcomes. That is why half of our forecasts take positions against consensus rather than shading toward the base rate. If the regime is changing, a base rate constructed from the prior regime is the wrong prior.

**Reason:** ruling — cut. Where a specific forecast leans against consensus, the derivation says so with the specific evidence; the general license to disagree with markets added assertion, not argument.

## 3. Page-18 verification note

**Removed:** the bracketed "*[Verify before submitting, then delete this note: …]*" block listing unverified data (Korea unmanned-store count, Meta MCI press figures, Thailand BEV share, $700B capex guidance, and the per-figure series).

**Reason:** ruling — it announced to reviewers that several datasets were unverified. The underlying verification was performed instead (see `verification/` and `CHANGELOG.md`); each listed item was either verified against a primary source, re-attributed as press reporting or estimate, or removed.

## 4. Korea unmanned-store claim (Part 2 §4)

**Removed:** "Korea's unmanned-shop count has grown into the tens of thousands."

**Reason:** listed as unverified in the draft's own note; no primary source was established during verification, and the Japan self-checkout series carries the automation-follows-scarcity point on its own.

## 5. "Enforcement bounty" attribution (original forecast 5 linkage and Part 2 §2)

**Removed:** the draft's phrasing attributing a "formalized enforcement bounty" to the export-control listing itself.

**Reason:** Phase 2 verification — the listing instrument (MOFCOM Announcement No. 23 of 2026, June 22) contains prohibitions and a licensing-exception process only. A reward mechanism for reporting export-control violations does exist, but as a separate instrument (Announcement No. 26 of 2026, effective July 1). The revised text cites the two instruments separately instead of conflating them. See `verification/V01_mofcom_mp_materials.md`.

## 6. AI Act August 2026 trigger (Part 2 §3 and Thread 2)

**Removed:** "The AI Act's general application from 2 August 2026 puts workplace AI systems in the high-risk tier from that date," and the AI Office listed as a qualifying authority on that basis.

**Reason:** Phase 2 verification — the Digital Omnibus moved the high-risk-system compliance timeline; the enforcement forecast now rests on GDPR, national labour law, and data-protection authorities only. See `verification/V02_eu_ai_enforcement.md`.

## 7. Original title and epigraph

**Removed:** title "Forecasting the Future 2026: Submission" (a filename, not a title) and the eighteen-way epigraph phrasing.

**Reason:** replaced by *Control Without Feedback: Forecasts on AI and Modern Mercantilism* per ruling; the pricing conceit survives as the Part 1 header.

## 8. USD/JPY, sector-return, Fed-funds, and LA Metro material in Part 2/Part 3

**Removed:** the funding-side tremor sentences (original 14), the term-premium and reaction-function passages (original 16), the value-migration-as-market-price framing (original 18), and the LA natural-experiment paragraph with its burn-up chart (original 3).
