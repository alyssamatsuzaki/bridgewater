## Claim 1: MP Materials appears on a MOFCOM export-control list (as of July 2026); which list, which announcement, still listed?

**Status: CONFIRMED** (via converging search snippets; primary MOFCOM page identified but not directly fetchable)

**What the sources say:**
On June 22, 2026, China's Ministry of Commerce (MOFCOM) issued **Announcement No. 23 of 2026** (商务部公告2026年第23号), titled "公布将10家美国实体列入出口管制管控名单的决定" ("Decision to Place 10 US Entities on the Export Control List"), adding MP Materials Corp. and nine other U.S. entities — USA Rare Earth, Aveox, Red Cat Holdings, Teal Drones, IMSAR, Jaia Robotics, Ball Aerospace & Technologies Corp, Oshkosh Defense, and L3Harris Maritime Services — to China's **出口管制管控名单 (Export Control List)**, issued pursuant to the PRC Export Control Law and the Regulations on Export Control of Dual-Use Items. MP Materials Corp. is listed with its corporate address (1700 S Pavilion Center Drive, 8th Floor, Las Vegas, NV).

This is **explicitly the Export Control List, not the separate "Unreliable Entity List" (不可靠实体清单)**. These are two distinct MOFCOM blacklist regimes in Chinese trade law; search results distinguish them clearly (a separate October 2025 action added 14 different entities to the Unreliable Entity List, unrelated to this MP Materials designation). No search result places MP Materials on the Unreliable Entity List.

MOFCOM said the move was a response to the U.S. expanding its "China military-industrial entity" list (1260H list) earlier in June 2026, and was framed as protecting national security and non-proliferation obligations.

**Current status (most recent evidence found):** A Morgan Lewis client alert dated July 2026 ("Recent China Export Control Actions Signal Active Enforcement for Rare Earths and Strategic Minerals") discusses this listing as part of an ongoing, actively-enforced regime, with no indication of removal or suspension. No source found reports MP Materials being delisted or the listing being suspended. (Note: a *different*, unrelated set of MOFCOM rare-earth export control notices — Nos. 55–62 of 2025, covering specific rare-earth elements — was suspended Nov 7, 2025–Nov 10, 2026 under a US-China trade truce; this suspension applies to those item-specific controls, not to the Announcement No. 23 entity listing of MP Materials, and search results do not indicate any overlap or reversal of the entity listing itself.)

## Claim 2: Does the listing regime include a "formalized enforcement bounty"? Reviewer says the announcement only describes prohibitions + an exception/licensing process.

**Status: PARTLY SUPPORTED** (reviewer is correct about Announcement No. 23 specifically; a genuine separate MOFCOM bounty mechanism does exist, but it is a different announcement, two days later, not part of the MP Materials listing decision)

**What Announcement No. 23 (the actual MP Materials listing) establishes — CONFIRMS the reviewer:**
Per converging search snippets, Announcement No. 23 of 2026 establishes only:
1. A prohibition on Chinese exporters supplying dual-use items to the ten listed entities (including MP Materials);
2. A prohibition on any organization or individual, anywhere, transferring or supplying China-origin dual-use goods to the listed entities;
3. A requirement that any ongoing relevant export transactions be suspended immediately;
4. An **exception/licensing process**: exporters "may apply to MOFCOM for approval in cases of genuine necessity" (i.e., a case-by-case license application route).

No search snippet describing Announcement No. 23's content mentions any reward, bounty, or whistleblower-payment provision. This matches the reviewer's characterization: the listing announcement itself is a prohibition-plus-licensing-exception instrument, not an enforcement-bounty instrument.

**Separate search for ANY primary source establishing a reward/bounty mechanism — result: one exists, but it is not the MP Materials listing announcement.**
Two days after the MP Materials listing, on **June 24, 2026**, MOFCOM issued **Announcement No. 26 of 2026** (商务部公告2026年第26号), titled "关于进一步完善战略矿产两用物项出口管制违法违规行为举报处理工作有关事项的公告" ("Announcement on Further Improving Matters Relating to the Handling of Reports of Illegal Acts Violating Export Controls on Dual-Use Strategic Mineral Items"), effective **July 1, 2026**. Multiple independent sources — Bloomberg, Mining.com, Table.Briefings, and several Chinese outlets reproducing what appears to be the operative announcement text — converge on the same description:
- Establishes formal reporting channels for suspected violations of strategic-mineral dual-use export controls (unlicensed exports, exceeding license scope, exporting prohibited items, etc. — one Chinese-language source specifies 13 categories of reportable conduct), via a hotline (010-12369) and an online portal;
- For **real-name** reports that MOFCOM verifies as substantiated ("对查证属实的"), MOFCOM "may, in accordance with relevant regulations, give rewards to the real-name whistleblower" (对查证属实的，商务部按有关规定可给予实名举报者奖励) — Bloomberg reports MOFCOM did not disclose specific reward amounts;
- Includes a confidentiality protection for reporters and a leniency provision for voluntary self-disclosure by exporters;
- Chinese commentary characterizes this as a shift from "after-the-fact penalty" to "whole-of-society supervision" (从"事后处罚"升级为"全民监督") and as a "报有奖+坦白从宽" (report-for-reward plus lenient treatment for confession) dual-track system.

**Conclusion on Claim 2:** A real, verifiable MOFCOM reward/bounty mechanism for export-control violation reporting does exist as of July 2026 — but it was created by **Announcement No. 26 of 2026 (June 24 / effective July 1, 2026)**, a distinct instrument from **Announcement No. 23 of 2026 (June 22, 2026)**, which is the one that actually placed MP Materials on the Export Control List and which contains only prohibitions plus a licensing-exception process, no bounty. If the manuscript's phrase "the listing regime includes a formalized enforcement bounty" is read as describing Announcement No. 23 itself, that is **CONTRADICTED** — the reviewer is right. If read more loosely as describing China's broader 2026 strategic-mineral export-control enforcement apparatus (of which the MP Materials listing is one part and the whistleblower-reward rule is a separate, contemporaneous part), the underlying fact of a bounty mechanism existing is **CONFIRMED**, but it should not be attributed to the MP Materials listing announcement itself. Recommend the manuscript cite Announcement No. 26, not No. 23, if retaining the bounty claim, and make clear it is a general strategic-minerals reporting/reward rule, not an MP-Materials-specific enforcement tool.
