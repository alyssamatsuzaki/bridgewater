## 2. FOMC July 28–29, 2026 meeting

**Status: CONFIRMED (target range, vote, dissent, "fifth consecutive"); PARTLY SUPPORTED (exact statement wording); CONFIRMED (no July SEP / dot-plot shift)**

### Target range, vote, dissents — CONFIRMED
- Federal Reserve Board press release, **"Federal Reserve issues FOMC statement,"** federalreserve.gov, July 29, 2026, 2:00 p.m. EDT. URL: https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/pressreleases/monetary20260729a.htm (channel: WebSearch synthesis of federalreserve.gov; direct fetch 403'd). Companion: **Implementation Note issued July 29, 2026**, URL: https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/pressreleases/monetary20260729a1.htm and PDF https://www.federalreserve.gov/monetarypolicy/files/monetary20260729a1.pdf (IOR held at 3.65%, primary credit rate at 3.75%, effective July 30, 2026).
- Corroborated by numerous reputable press outlets, all dated July 29–30, 2026: CNBC ("Fed rate decision July 2026: Divided Fed holds interest rates steady," cnbc.com/2026/07/29/fed-rate-decision-july-2026.html), Bloomberg ("Fed Holds Rates Steady as Three Officials Dissent in Favor of a Hike," 2026-07-29), CNN Business ("Fed holds interest rates steady after cliffhanger meeting, but three officials dissent," 2026-07-29), NPR ("A divided Federal Reserve holds interest rates steady despite high inflation," npr.org/2026/07/29), U.S. News ("Fed Holds Rates Steady, but 3 Members Favored a Rate Hike," 2026-07-29), and the Boston Globe/AP wire. Channel: reputable press (secondary), consistent across outlets.
- **Target range: held at 3.50%–3.75%.** CONFIRMED, unanimous across all sources.
- **Fifth consecutive meeting at this range.** CONFIRMED — explicitly stated (CBS News, citing FactSet-polled economists' expectation that proved correct: "That marked the fifth consecutive meeting when the central bank left its benchmark rate unchanged").
- **Vote: 9–3.** CONFIRMED across CNBC, Bloomberg, NPR, CNN, U.S. News, Detroit News, TechTimes.
- **Three dissents preferring +25bp hike, named:** Beth M. Hammack (Cleveland Fed), Neel Kashkari (Minneapolis Fed), Lorie K. Logan (Dallas Fed) — all three "preferred to raise the target range for the federal funds rate by ¼ percentage point at this meeting." CONFIRMED, named identically across every outlet checked. Multiple outlets additionally note this was **the first time since September 2016 that three policymakers dissented with a unified directional view**, and that new Fed Chair Kevin Warsh remarked "I asked for a good family fight, and I got one" (per Reuters-sourced synthesis).

### Statement language on inflation/price stability — PARTLY SUPPORTED
Search synthesis of the federalreserve.gov statement text yielded: "Inflation remains elevated relative to the Committee's 2 percent goal, in part reflecting supply shocks that have driven price increases in certain sectors, including energy," alongside "Economic activity is expanding at a solid pace despite elevated uncertainty that owes, in part, to the conflict in the Middle East," and "in support of the Federal Reserve's dual mandate." These phrases recurred consistently across independent search queries, giving moderate confidence they reflect actual statement language. One synthesis also produced the line "The Committee will deliver price stability" attributed to the statement — I could **not** corroborate this specific phrase in a second independent pull, and it reads atypically for FOMC statement boilerplate, so I flag it as **COULD NOT VERIFY / possible synthesis artifact** rather than a confirmed direct quote. Because I could not directly fetch and read the full primary statement (403), I cannot certify the complete statement text or confirm no material clauses are missing/mischaracterized.

### SEP question — CONFIRMED: no July SEP; last SEP was June 17, 2026, and it had already dropped the previously projected cut in favor of a hike
- The Federal Reserve publishes the Summary of Economic Projections on a quarterly cadence tied to four of the eight annual FOMC meetings (March, June, September, December). July is **not** an SEP meeting. Confirmed via search results showing SEP releases at **March 18, 2026** (federalreserve.gov/monetarypolicy/fomcprojtabl20260318.htm) and **June 17, 2026** (federalreserve.gov/monetarypolicy/fomcprojtabl20260617.htm and fomcprojtabl20260617.pdf), with the next SEP tied to the **September 15–16, 2026** meeting. No July 2026 SEP exists.
- **March 2026 SEP** (channel: WebSearch synthesis of federalreserve.gov/press coverage): median federal-funds-rate projection for end-2026 was **3.4%**, i.e., implying roughly one 25bp **cut** from the (then-prevailing) 3.50–3.75% range, with a second cut penciled in for 2027. Same SEP: 2026 GDP growth 2.4%, unemployment 4.4%, headline and core PCE inflation both 2.7%.
- **June 2026 SEP** (channel: WebSearch synthesis, corroborated by TD Economics and CBS News/Yahoo Finance summaries): core PCE inflation for 2026 was revised **up** to 3.3% (from 2.7%), GDP growth revised **down** to 2.2% (from 2.4%), and — critically — "the full committee in June penciled in **one quarter-percentage-point increase** by the end of 2026" (CBS News/Yahoo synthesis), i.e., the median dot flipped from a projected cut (March) to a projected **hike** (June).
- **Conclusion on the lead's SEP question: CONFIRMED that the committee's projections had, by the last SEP (June 17, 2026), already dropped the previously projected cut** — this happened at the June SEP, not "in July," because there was no July SEP. Any manuscript language implying a July SEP event should be corrected to reference the June 17, 2026 SEP as the vehicle for that shift.

## 5. Market-implied odds of a Fed hike by end-2026, "roughly even," late July 2026

**Status: PARTLY SUPPORTED — figure is highly date-sensitive and inconsistent across sources; "roughly even" fits only part of the relevant window**

I was not able to query the CME FedWatch tool itself (cmegroup.com) directly — all WebFetch attempts in this session 403'd regardless of domain (proxy-level issue, confirmed via `$HTTPS_PROXY/__agentproxy/status`). All figures below come via WebSearch synthesis/press summaries and should be treated as **secondary, and in several cases from lower-reliability aggregator or prediction-market sites** rather than CME Group itself. I explicitly label reliability below.

Data points found, in rough chronological order (all 2026):
- **~June 11, 2026** (KuCoin news flash, channel: crypto-press secondary, low-to-moderate reliability): "CME FedWatch: 69% Probability of a Fed Rate Hike in 2026."
- **Mid-July 2026** (Polymarket contract "Fed rate hike in 2026?," channel: prediction market, secondary): priced around **52.5% Yes / 47.5% No** on ~$4.1M volume — described in synthesis as "near a coin-flip," i.e., closest match to the "roughly even" characterization in the lead.
- **May 15, 2026** (CNBC, "Traders now see next Fed interest rate move as a hike following inflation surge," channel: reputable press): reported a December-2026 hike probability of **~51%** via CME FedWatch, and July-meeting hike odds of **46.5%** as of mid-July per a separate CNBC piece ("A July rate hike from the Fed? The odds are rising," 2026-07-13).
- **~3 weeks before July 29 decision** (i.e., ~July 8, per an X/Twitter post citing "Investrade," channel: social media, low reliability — flagged, not fully trusted): CME FedWatch shown at **~87%** probability of a hike by year-end 2026, attributed to hawkish FOMC minutes and Middle East-driven inflation concerns.
- **July 23, 2026** (Forbes, "Markets Price In Rising Odds Of July Fed Rate Hike," channel: reputable press): odds of a hike specifically at the July meeting were rising ahead of the decision (this is meeting-specific, not year-end).
- **July 28, 2026** (Polymarket, channel: prediction market, secondary): reported at **78%** for a 2026 hike overall, with September-meeting odds at 70% and October at 74% — i.e., no longer "roughly even," tilted clearly toward yes.
- **Post-decision, July 29–30, 2026** (Bloomberg, "Fed Funds Futures Shorts Unwind After Fed Holds Rates Steady" and "Fed Holds Rates Steady, Treasury Yields Shift as September Hike Bets Ease," channel: reputable press): interest-rate swaps showed **~60% probability of a September hike** immediately after the hold (down from higher pre-meeting readings as some short positions unwound), with one synthesis describing a December hike as "fully priced in" (i.e., near-certainty, not "roughly even") by very late July.
- One aggregator (growbeansprout.com, channel: low-reliability secondary) separately claimed a **54.4%** probability of a **cut** at the September 16, 2026 meeting as of July 28 — this **contradicts** the hike-leaning figures from Bloomberg/Polymarket/CNBC found elsewhere and appears inconsistent with the broader corroborated narrative (inflation running hot, three hawkish dissents, rising yields). I flag this data point as likely unreliable or reflecting a different, non-comparable metric, and do not rely on it.

**Assessment:** The claim that odds of a hike by end-2026 were "roughly even" as of late July 2026 is **only partly supported**. A genuinely close-to-50/50 reading (52.5%) appears in a mid-July Polymarket snapshot, which is consistent with "roughly even." However, multiple other readings from the same general window (May CNBC/CME FedWatch ~51% for December specifically — also close to even and supportive) sit alongside readings from immediately before and after the July 29 decision (CME-attributed ~87% via a social-media post, Polymarket 78%, Bloomberg's "fully priced in" characterization for December) that are well above "roughly even" and instead show odds tilted meaningfully toward a hike. Given the news flow around the meeting (hot inflation, oil-driven, three hawkish dissents, 30-year yield breaking out), a shift from "near coin-flip" toward "hike increasingly expected" over the second half of July is the more internally consistent read across sources. A manuscript citing "roughly even" for **late July specifically** should be treated with caution; "roughly even to modestly hike-leaning earlier in July, shifting toward more confidently hike-leaning by the July 29 decision and after" would better fit the weight of evidence gathered here. Recommend the manuscript cite a single, dated CME FedWatch snapshot (ideally pulled directly from cmegroup.com, which I could not access in this session) rather than the "roughly even" characterization as currently framed.
